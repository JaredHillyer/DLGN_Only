# Neural Cellular Automata at MarrLab and the DLCA Bridge

Across the 2020–present window, the strongest through-line is that entity["organization","Marr Lab","helmholtz munich group"] has not primarily used NCAs for inverse rule discovery in the Schumacher sense; instead, it has pushed NCAs as *small, robust, medically useful iterative local processors* for microscopy classification, weakly supervised segmentation, and, through immediate collaborators at entity["organization","Helmholtz Munich","biomedical research center"] and entity["organization","Technical University of Munich","munich university"], temporally consistent MRI synthesis. The lab-level claim is not “NCAs are universal models of biology,” but closer to: *local, recurrent, parameter-efficient update rules can outperform or complement conventional CNNs when robustness, explainability, weak supervision, or temporal consistency matter more than sheer benchmark scale*. fileciteturn0file0 citeturn13view0turn18search6turn18search7turn18search4turn39search2turn39search3

## DLCA and DiffLogic CA

entity["article","Differentiable Logic Cellular Automata: From Game of Life to Pattern Generation","arxiv 2025"] is best read as a **bridge paper** between two previously separate lines: NCA as trainable local recurrence and DLGN as trainable discrete logic circuits. The prerequisite DLGN paper, entity["article","Deep Differentiable Logic Gate Networks","neurips 2022"], establishes differentiable logic gates and reports discretized inference faster than one million MNIST images per second on a single CPU core. The convolutional extension, entity["article","Convolutional Differentiable Logic Gate Networks","neurips 2024"], scales the paradigm, reporting 86.29% CIFAR-10 accuracy with 61 million logic gates and a 29× size reduction versus its stated comparison baseline. DLCA then places that discrete-logic idea inside a recurrent local-update cellular substrate. citeturn9search2turn9search3turn19view2

What DLCA has **actually** been demonstrated on is narrow but clear: exact recovery of entity["video_game","Conway's Game of Life","cellular automaton"] rules, checkerboard generation with resilience to noise and damage, lizard-shape growth, and multi-color pattern generation. The official paper and project page also state that, for simpler patterns, both synchronous and asynchronous updates succeed, and that the model shows nontrivial generalization and perturbation robustness. citeturn19view2turn12search1turn38search7

The most important negative finding is that the public sources do **not** show DLCA already delivering the full NCA package. They show discrete local-rule learning on relatively small visual tasks; they do **not** yet show medical imaging, real biological data, stochastic process modeling, Schumacher-style trajectory system identification, or rigorous pre/post-discretization behavioral audits across difficult tasks. The official project page even says that more complex shapes and structures remain challenging and proposes hierarchical NCAs or forgetting mechanisms as future improvements. citeturn12search1turn38search7

On the “soft-to-discrete snap,” the public abstract is encouraging but incomplete. It explicitly says training is fully differentiable and inference is fully discrete, meaning the model is deliberately trained in a soft relaxation and then run as a discrete circuit. That is the central promise of the paper. But I did **not** find, in the citable public snippets I retrieved, a dedicated quantitative ablation that measures behavioral degradation from soft training dynamics to snapped discrete inference across all tasks. So the correct status is: **the paper demonstrates that fully discrete inference works on its milestones, but a general-purpose discretization-error study is not publicly visible in the sources I could verify.** citeturn19view2turn12search1

On hardware, the picture is asymmetrical. DLGN is visibly hardware-aligned from the start: logic-gate inference, very high CPU throughput, and a public library geared toward sparse logic circuits. Related downstream LGN work then pushes verification and lighter parameterizations. But I did **not** find evidence that a trained DLCA model has already been synthesized to FPGA or ASIC in the literature I could verify, nor did I find published DLCA gate-count or power measurements. The closest adjacent hardware-native line I found was BitLogic in 2026, which exports LUT-native differentiable models to synthesizable HDL and reports sub-20 ns single-sample FPGA inference with fewer than 0.3M logic gates on CIFAR-10; however, BitLogic is not DLGN and not cellular automata, so it should be treated as **evidence of hardware feasibility for the broader design space, not proof of DLCA deployment**. citeturn9search2turn12search2turn38search2turn38search4turn38search6turn11search8

As of the report date, I did **not** find a clearly identifiable non-Google follow-on paper that takes DLCA itself into a new application domain. I found related LGN papers on verification and parameterization, but not a second-wave DLCA application paper. That silence is meaningful: the DLCA line is still at the “proof of concept” phase rather than the “branching ecosystem” phase. citeturn38search2turn38search4turn38search6turn38search7

Relative to classical NCA properties, the carry-over status is mixed. **Carried over:** local shared update rule, recurrence, asynchronous operation on simple tasks, robustness to some perturbations/noise, and explicit learnability of discrete local rules. **Not yet convincingly demonstrated:** regenerative robustness on complex targets, medical-domain generalization, stochastic dynamics, weak supervision, large-scale temporal system identification, and the kinds of strong scale-generalization claims made in Med-NCA or active NCA. That is exactly why DLCA is interesting for your agenda: the *structural* bridge exists, but the *application* bridge does not. citeturn19view2turn12search1turn4search3turn31search0

## Intersection and gap analysis

The key answer here is simple: **I found no published DLCA work on medical imaging or biological data, and I found no published DLCA work doing system identification from observed spatiotemporal data in the Schumacher or Sottoriva sense.** There is also no published head-to-head NCA versus DLCA comparison on equivalent medical or biological tasks that would let you make evidence-based claims about parameter budget, interpretability, or robustness. Any statement stronger than that would be overreach. citeturn19view2turn38search7turn5search1turn8search2

The practical consequence is that the NCA→DLCA translation is strongest where the task is already close to “learn a local discrete rule on a grid.” It gets progressively weaker as you move toward noisy microscopy, weak supervision, temporal irregular sampling, or latent stochasticity. MarrLab’s static classification papers are therefore **application-motivating** but not yet the best insertion points for DLCA. The best insertion points are TeNCA, Schumacher’s trajectory learning, and Sottoriva’s mixture/stochastic branch, because those are already asking for interpretable local dynamics rather than just compact classifiers. citeturn39search3turn5search1turn8search2

## Open research questions

1. **Can DLCA identify local update rules from observed PDE-like image trajectories?** → This is the direct Schumacher-style test of whether logic circuits can replace continuous NCA updates for inverse dynamics. → Needed: synthetic PDE benchmarks, trajectory losses, and a direct NCA vs DLCA comparison on rule recovery and rollout fidelity. citeturn5search1turn19view2

2. **Can DLCA handle continuous-valued medical images without destroying its interpretability advantage?** → DLCA is currently strongest on discrete/toy pattern tasks, while MarrLab and TeNCA operate on continuous microscopy/MRI data. → Needed: principled input encoders, possibly multi-bit or learned quantization, and discrete-vs-continuous ablations. citeturn39search3turn13view0

3. **Does snapping from soft training to discrete inference preserve long-horizon dynamics on nontrivial tasks?** → If not, DLCA’s core promise weakens exactly where system identification matters. → Needed: explicit pre/post-discretization trajectory-divergence studies, not just endpoint visuals. citeturn19view2turn12search1

4. **Can stochastic DLCA be built by importing mixture ideas from MNCA?** → Many biological systems are not governed by a single deterministic local rule. → Needed: mixture-of-circuits or probabilistic gate-selection mechanisms, plus stochastic-growth benchmarks. citeturn8search2

5. **Can DLCA support irregularly sampled time steps the way TeNCA does?** → Real clinical trajectories are temporally sparse and nonuniform. → Needed: time-conditioned or variable-step logic updates, plus DCE-MRI or other irregularly sampled sequence data. citeturn39search0turn39search3

6. **Can a hierarchical DLCA close the expressivity gap on medical classification and segmentation?** → hNCA’s main lesson is that hierarchy matters. → Needed: multiresolution logic-CA architectures and benchmarks on the same microscopy datasets used by hNCA/aNCA. citeturn18search7turn18search4turn12search1

The deepest conclusion from these silences is not “DLCA will obviously replace NCA.” It is narrower and more useful: **the literature has already established that NCAs can serve as compact local dynamical models, and that logic-gate networks can serve as trainable discrete circuits, but it has not yet established that trainable *discrete local circuits* can recover medically relevant or biologically meaningful spatiotemporal rules from data.** That is the most credible novel direction exposed by this survey. citeturn5search1turn19view2turn9search2turn9search3
